Text or live voice goes in. A controller decides which of 47 facial muscles and 5 jaw actuators fire, and a soft-tissue simulation turns those forces into skin motion. The same muscle signals drive a simulated head, ARKit avatars, Live2D, and Blender.

Lip error is the per-frame maximum over lip vertices (the MeshTalk/FaceFormer LVE), averaged over frames. The emotion judge is a classifier on expression geometry trained on real faces and their muscle fits. Real tracked faces close on fewer bilabials than the threshold expects because the tracker smooths fast lip contacts.

The body

52 actuators on a soft-tissue face

The face is MediaPipe's 468-vertex topology, so every frame tracked from any video corresponds vertex-for-vertex to the simulated skin. The rest shape is the measured average neutral face of 78 speakers.

Each muscle has a bony origin, a skin insertion, a FACS action unit and a maximal excursion. The solver adds skin elasticity, volume conservation (cheeks bulge, lips thicken), lip and eyelid contact, eyelids sliding on the eyes, force-length saturation, and a temporomandibular jaw whose forward glide was calibrated from real video.

Muscle motion capture

31 hours of ordinary video, turned into muscle activations

A physics-decoder autoencoder fits muscle activations to every tracked frame: the decoder is the differentiable surrogate of the simulator, so the only way to reconstruct a face is to find muscles that physically produce it. Mean lip residual across all clips: 0.81 mm, compared with 3.85 mm for a neutral face.

The decompositions read like FACS: disgust uses the nose wrinkler, sadness the chin raiser, anger the lip-corner depressor.

Three data problems were found and fixed. GRID's transcript folders are mislabeled for 13+ speakers. The CREMA-D video mirror has no audio. And lip contact does not transfer between faces, which made 18% of ground-truth frames interpenetrate until contact-preserving transfer was added.
Reinforcement learning

Learning to articulate with 52 muscles

The policy commands muscle excitations through first-order activation dynamics to reach a new mouth target every 100 ms. Same network, same environment, two learners.

  • No movement: 4.04 mm error
  • PPO, 3 M steps: 1.62 mm; 20 M steps: 1.86 mm
  • Analytic policy gradient through the muscle face, 3 M steps: 0.31 mm

The analytic gradient matched PPO's best training error after 55 k steps, 57× fewer. For overactuated robot faces, this favors learning on a calibrated differentiable muscle model, then transferring.

Using it

Avatars, VTubers, Blender

mimetic say "[happy:0.8] Hello!" -o hello.mp4: text with emotion tags to video. Kokoro TTS gives exact phoneme timing.

mimetic live --vmc / --vts: microphone to VSeeFace, Warudo, VNyan (52 ARKit blendshapes) or VTube Studio (Live2D parameters).

mimetic export: Live Link Face CSV, JSON, OBJ + PC2 point cache. The Blender add-on imports the head with jaw, teeth, eyes and voice, or keyframes ARKit shape keys on your own character.

Live mode: a causal convolutional network with 80 ms lookahead. On held-out speakers it reaches — mm lip error with — of bilabials sealed, inferring emotion from the voice.

Limits

What it does not do yet

  • The face is a single 468-vertex shell with procedural teeth, tongue and eyes. It has no wrinkles and no articulated tongue.
  • Ground truth comes from a monocular tracker, which under-reports fast lip contacts and depth.
  • Emotional training speech is acted, from 14 sentences. Generalization was measured on new TTS sentences, not on real recordings of them.
  • RAVDESS is non-commercial, so this model is for research only. Each clip records its source, so retraining on GRID + CREMA-D only is possible.
